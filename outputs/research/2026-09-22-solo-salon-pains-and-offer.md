# Solo salon pains and a founding offer they won't blink at

Research date: 22 September 2026. Companion to `2026-09-22-booking-competitors.md` (read that for competitor pricing and complaints, it isn't repeated here).

How this was done: web searches only. Reddit, Facebook groups, Hellopeter, News24, MyBroadband, Pro Beauty SA and most trade sites were blocked from this machine, so I couldn't open threads and pull word-for-word quotes the way I wanted. What's here comes from search-result summaries of those pages. Where I quote, it's the wording the search summary gave, so treat it as close but not guaranteed exact. Anything I couldn't confirm is marked **(unverified)**. Anything I worked out myself is marked **(estimate)**. Dollar figures are converted at about R18.

---

## 1. Short version

Two pains carry almost all the money: the hours lost to WhatsApp back-and-forth ("how much?", "are you open Saturday?", "send pics") and the bookings lost to no-shows, late cancels and slow replies. For a braider, one no-show can wipe out half a day's income, because a 5-hour slot can't be refilled at short notice.

R1,000 a month does pay for itself for a typical solo braider, but only just, and only if you count her time. For a 2-3 chair salon it pays for itself two or three times over. The catch is on Theo's side: at full volume a busy 3-chair salon could cost him R600 to R900 a month to run, so R999 flat is thin there.

The offer that removes the hesitation isn't really about the price. It's about who carries the risk. My recommendation: **R0 setup, and they pay nothing until Lexi has booked their first 20 clients.** After that it's R999 a month, month to month, cancel with one WhatsApp, price locked while they stay. Offer a R599 solo version for one-chair operators. Theo collects everything in one 15-minute visit and they're live in 4 days.

Two things came up in the research that Theo should know before he pitches anyone:

- **Meta changes WhatsApp pricing on 1 October 2026.** Every reply Lexi sends will cost about 12c, but each number gets 1,000 free replies a month. A solo operator will almost never go over. A busy salon will. Details in section 4.
- **Keeping the salon's existing number on both the WhatsApp Business app and Lexi at the same time ("coexistence") may not work for +27 numbers.** Earlier 2026 sources say South Africa was excluded, one July 2026 source says it's just been added **(unverified, check in Meta's signup flow before promising "your own number")**.

## 2. Top 10 pains, ranked

Ranked by how often the pain showed up in what I found, then by what it costs. Cost figures are for a solo braider unless noted, and they're all **estimates** built from the assumptions in section 3.

### 1. Answering the same WhatsApp questions all day

"How much for knotless, waist length?" "Are you open Saturday?" "Send pics." "Where are you?" "Do I bring my own hair?" The same five questions, dozens of times a week, most from people who never book.

- **Cost (estimate):** 45 to 90 minutes a day, so 5 to 8 hours a week. Worth roughly R400 to R800 a week of her time at R80 an hour.
- **Evidence:** Booking-tool vendors put solo beauty admin at "5 or more hours per week", with 6 to 8 hours for solo stylists ([Solo booking system](https://booksolo.co/my-business/features/booking-system), [Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/stop-losing-bookings-stylists-busy)). These are vendor numbers, so they lean high. A hairstylist-business article warns that "non-stop back and forth wastes time and prevents stylists from having clients in their chair" ([Dítē](https://dite.ca/how-to-handle-client-dms-as-a-hairstylist/)). SA Facebook groups are full of "what does a price list look like" and pricing threads ([Nail tech SA Support](https://www.facebook.com/groups/NailtechSa/posts/27722566777434886/)), which I couldn't open.
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: answers prices, services and availability. **New idea:** a "starter pack" she sends automatically to first-time askers: price list image, 6 best photos, address pin, what to bring. One message replaces the five questions.

### 2. No-shows and late cancellations

- **Cost (estimate):** 2 missed appointments a month at R550 = R1,100 a month for a braider. For a 3-chair salon at 11% missed, about 22 appointments a month, maybe R3,000 to R6,000 lost.
- **Evidence:** The best SA number I found: Pro Beauty South Africa reports salons with an 8% cancellation rate and 3% no-show rate, so **11% of booked appointments are missed**, with nail salons at 16% cancellations ([Pro Beauty SA](https://www.probeauty.co.za/repeat-clients-generate-80-of-beauty-salon-revenue/)). International figures run higher, 10% to 30% ([SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/hub/scheduling/appointment-no-show-statistics), [Heygoldie](https://heygoldie.com/blog/how-to-calculate-salon-no-show-rate)). For braids, Fresha shows Cape Town sessions running 4.5 to 6.5 hours ([Fresha Cape Town braiding](https://www.fresha.com/lp/en/tt/hair-braiding/in/za-cape-town)), so one no-show is half a day or more with nothing to fill it. The whole braiding world runs on non-refundable deposits because of this ([Hairnergy booking policy](https://hairnergybraids.com/pages/booking-policy), [NBC News on stylists vs clients](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/black-hair-industry-stylists-frustration-instagram-tiktok-rcna139041)).
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: reminders. Planned (competitor doc copy list): reminder with Confirm / Reschedule / Cancel buttons, deposits by payment link, a no-show list so only repeat offenders get asked for a deposit.

### 3. Missing or slow replies, so the client books someone else

Her hands are in someone's hair for 5 hours. By the time she looks at her phone, the enquiry has gone cold.

- **Cost (estimate):** 1 to 2 lost bookings a month for a solo operator (R550 to R1,100). 4 to 6 for a small salon.
- **Evidence:** Roughly 40% to 52% of salon bookings are made outside opening hours ([San Francisco Oy data via Cision](https://news.cision.com/san-francisco-oy/r/new-data--over-50--of-beauty-and-hair-salon-customers-book-their-appointment-outside-the-opening-hou,c3463133), [Simple Salon](https://www.simplesalon.com/online-booking-roi-statistics-salon-owners/)). Vendors claim the average solo pro takes 4 to 6 hours to reply and a third of messages go unanswered ([Professional Beauty UK](https://professionalbeauty.co.uk/how-solo-beauty-pros-earn-20k-extra-convert-enquiries), **unverified**, I couldn't open it). A lash salon owner quoted by a booking vendor: she was "losing clients because I couldn't always answer the phone in my spa" ([Zenoti lash apps](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/best-lash-appointment-booking-apps)). 57% of clients who switch provider name difficulty booking as a reason ([Square research via Thriving Stylist](https://thrivingstylist.com/podcast-transcriptions/307/)).
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: replies day and night. This only works once the rebuild gets replies under 20 seconds.

### 4. Chasing deposits and checking proof of payment

"Did you send it?" "Send POP." Then logging into the banking app to check it really landed, because fake proof-of-payment screenshots are a known SA scam.

- **Cost (estimate):** 1 to 2 hours a week, plus the occasional fake POP (one lost R550 slot every few months).
- **Evidence:** Absa, Nedbank and Standard Bank all warn businesses about fake proof of payment sent over WhatsApp ([Absa on X](https://x.com/AbsaSouthAfrica/status/1725182368909045773), [Standard Bank](https://www.businessonline.standardbank.co.za/bolsa/businessonline/security-centre/deposit-refund-scams), [SD Law](https://www.sdlaw.co.za/articles/eft-scam-in-south-africa/)). Deposits are the main tool every competitor sells (see competitor doc).
- **Lexi fix:** Planned: Yoco or PayFast payment link, and Lexi only confirms the booking when the payment actually clears. No screenshots to check. Yoco links cost 2.95% ex VAT, about R5 on a R150 deposit ([Yoco gateway](https://www.yoco.com/za/gateway/)).

### 5. Ghosting after the price, and haggling

She types out the price, the person reads it and vanishes. Or asks for "a special". Either way, 10 minutes gone.

- **Cost (estimate):** part of pain #1's hours. Maybe 3 enquiries for every booking.
- **Evidence:** A hairstylist-coaching podcast covers the "clients ghosting trend": people who ask, get a booking link, and disappear, happening to "successful stylists with established brands" ([Thriving Stylist #307](https://thrivingstylist.com/podcast/307/)). The Free State hair salon study found over half of surveyed salons had daily sales under R300 and said salons "can't increase prices anymore because their clients can't afford it" ([DHET hairdressing report](https://www.dhet.gov.za/Skills%20on%20Hairdressing%20SA/Project%20Hair_Economic%20Report_17112016.pdf), [Free State study](https://www.psef.org.za/sites/default/files/7%20Ms%20van%20Rooyen-Kruger%20HAIR%20SALON%20INDUSTRY%20A%20CONTRIBUTOR%20TO%20THE%20FREE%20STATE%20ECONOMY_0.pdf)), so price pressure is real. No direct SA quotes on haggling found.
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: gives the price instantly, so the owner never spends time on it. **New idea:** one polite follow-up the next day ("Still keen for Saturday? I've got 9am open"). Must be inside the 24-hour window to stay a cheap service message. Lexi never discounts; anything that sounds like negotiation goes to the owner.

### 6. Replying at night, losing family time, burning out

- **Cost:** hard to put in Rand. Evenings and Sundays on the phone.
- **Evidence:** A L'Oréal Professionnel survey of 1,750 hair professionals found 65% had experienced anxiety, depression or burnout ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/l-or-al-professionnel-launches-free-mental-health-resource-for-hairdressers-209070)). A Pro Beauty Association study found 53% of hair pros reply to clients immediately and 49% regularly stay late ([Pro Beauty Association](https://www.probeauty.org/hustle-culture-sucks-a-study-on-balance-culture-in-hair-professionals/)). Advice to stylists: once you answer a 2am DM, "clients will expect it forever" ([Dítē](https://dite.ca/how-to-handle-client-dms-as-a-hairstylist/)). Lash artists describe the job as "part art, part science, and part emotional support hotline" ([Stacy Lash](https://stacylash.com/blogs/client-concerns/burnout-in-the-lash-industry-how-to-recognize-it-and-reignite-your-passion)). These are mostly US/UK samples.
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: she answers so the owner doesn't have to. Planned: a morning WhatsApp summary ("Today: 2 clients, both confirmed, 1 deposit paid") so the owner checks one message instead of 40.

### 7. Double bookings and the paper diary

- **Cost (estimate):** one clash every month or two. Each one means a refund, an angry client, sometimes a bad review.
- **Evidence:** Mostly indirect. Clients complain about stylists who "double and triple book" ([Lipstick Alley thread](https://www.lipstickalley.com/threads/professional-braiders-are-non-existent-poor-customer-service-500-for-10-braids-ikyfl-rant.5304490/)). It's in Theo's own pitch from talking to salons (`context/offer.md`).
- **Lexi fix:** Existing: live Google Calendar check. Planned: reschedule and cancel by chat.

### 8. Getting new clients and Google reviews

- **Cost (estimate):** each new regular is worth R3,000 to R6,000 a year to a braider (every 6 to 8 weeks at R550).
- **Evidence:** 88% of people read reviews before booking a salon and 74% use Google Search to find one ([BrightLocal 2026 via Taylor Scher SEO](https://www.taylorscherseo.com/blog/local-seo-statistics), [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). The State of the SMME report says 56.9% of SA small businesses see "access to markets" as the thing that would help them grow most ([Sunday Times](https://www.sundaytimes.timeslive.co.za/business/2026-09-17-access-to-customers-barrier-for-smme-growth/)). In townships, word of mouth is still the most trusted source ([Bizcommunity township report](https://www.bizcommunity.com/Article/196/168/228947.html)).
- **Lexi fix:** Planned: Google review request after a confirmed visit. The website. **New idea:** set up or clean up their Google Business Profile during onboarding with a "Book on WhatsApp" button. It costs Theo 20 minutes and is often the thing that actually brings strangers in.

### 9. Clients drift away and don't rebook

- **Cost (estimate):** if 3 regulars a month forget to come back on time, that's R1,650 a month for a braider.
- **Evidence:** Repeat clients bring in 80% of salon and spa revenue while being 42% of clients ([Pro Beauty SA](https://www.probeauty.co.za/repeat-clients-generate-80-of-beauty-salon-revenue/)).
- **Lexi fix:** Planned: rebooking nudge ("It's been 6 weeks since your braids"). These are marketing messages at about R0.62 each from Meta, so only send to people who said yes to them.

### 10. Load shedding, data and phone problems

- **Cost:** varies a lot. Lower priority right now, because load shedding has been much lighter since 2024 **(unverified for 2026)**.
- **Evidence:** During load shedding beauticians reported earning "a third of the income" they had before, and clients only booked when they had power ([Pro Beauty SA](https://www.probeauty.co.za/salon-businesses-severely-affected-by-load-shedding/), [UN South Africa](https://southafrica.un.org/en/233275-impact-load-shedding-livelihoods-story-two-resilient-single-mums), [TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2022-09-22-i-feel-like-crying-sometimes-how-load-shedding-is-crippling-small-businesses/)). Prepaid data is still about R79 for 1GB ([Daily News](https://dailynews.co.za/business-report/opinion/2025-10-07-south-africans-catch-a-break-with-reduced-data-costs/)). I found nothing specific on phone storage filling up with client photos, though it's a common complaint in general.
- **Lexi fix:** Existing (and worth saying in the pitch): Lexi runs in the cloud, so she keeps booking when the owner's phone is dead, out of data or the power's off. **New idea:** a "move everyone" button. If the power goes, the owner sends Lexi one message and Lexi offers every client that day a new time.

**What I couldn't find:** real data on how many enquiries a Cape Flats braider gets a day, SA-specific no-show rates for braiding, anything on Afrikaans-speaking salon clients, or direct quotes from Cape Town operators. Theo's first 5 visits are the best source for all of this. Worth asking every prospect: "How many WhatsApps did you get yesterday? How many no-shows last month?" and writing it down.

## 3. Does R1,000 a month pay for itself?

Everything here is an **estimate**. Change the numbers to match the owner in front of you.

### Solo braider (home-based, Mitchells Plain or Kuils River)

Assumptions:

- Works 6 days, about 8 clients a week, so 35 appointments a month
- Average ticket R550 (Fresha shows R250 to R1,250 in Cape Town, average around R550 in the City Centre, Cape Flats likely a bit lower)
- Monthly takings: about R19,000. Profit after hair, products and transport, maybe R8,000 to R11,000. The Free State study found 85% of salons made under R10,000 profit a month, which fits.
- Her time is worth R80 an hour (R550 over roughly 5 hours, rounded down because not all hours can be sold)
- 6 hours a week on WhatsApp admin now. Lexi takes about 70% of it.
- 2 missed appointments a month now. Reminders with confirm buttons save 1 of them.
- Lexi wins 1 extra booking a month from after-hours replies she'd have missed.

| What Lexi saves | Per month |
|---|---|
| Time: 4.2 hrs a week x 4.3 weeks = 18 hrs x R80 | R1,440 (time, not cash) |
| One no-show avoided | R550 |
| One extra booking from late-night replies | R550 |
| **Cash back in her pocket** | **R1,100** |
| **Cash plus time** | **R2,540** |

**Verdict:** At R999 she's R100 up in pure cash, R1,540 up if you count her time. It pays for itself, but it's close enough that she'll feel it, because R999 could be 10% of her monthly profit. At R599 she's clearly ahead on cash alone, which is the whole case for a solo tier. The number that sells it is the no-show: "One client not pitching costs you R550. Lexi costs R20 a day."

### Small 2-3 chair salon (Parow or Bellville)

Assumptions:

- 3 chairs, about 200 appointments a month, average ticket R300, so about R60,000 takings
- 15 hours a week of WhatsApp admin between the owner and stylists. Lexi takes 10.
- 11% missed (Pro Beauty SA rate) = 22 a month. Reminders save a third, so 7.
- 5 extra bookings a month from faster and after-hours replies
- Staff time valued at R60 an hour

| What Lexi saves | Per month |
|---|---|
| Time: 10 hrs a week x 4.3 = 43 hrs x R60 | R2,580 (time) |
| 7 missed appointments saved x R300 | R2,100 |
| 5 extra bookings x R300 | R1,500 |
| **Cash** | **R3,600** |
| **Cash plus time** | **R6,180** |

**Verdict:** Easy yes. R999 is under 2% of takings and pays back 3.6 times in cash. This is the customer where Theo is underpriced, not overpriced (see his costs below).

## 4. Theo's real costs per client

All **estimates**. Worth checking against the first month's actual Make and Anthropic bills.

### What changed with WhatsApp pricing

From **1 October 2026**, Meta charges for every reply a business sends on the WhatsApp API, about 10c to 15c each in South Africa. Each phone number gets **1,000 free replies a month**, then it's charged from the 1,001st. Utility templates (like reminders) sent inside the 24-hour window also become billable. The ordinary WhatsApp Business phone app stays free ([Briefly](https://briefly.co.za/people/250520-meta-charge-whatsapp-business-api-users-message-1-october-2026/), [Bandwidth Blog](https://bandwidthblog.co.za/2026/08/19/meta-whatsapp-south-africa-business-cost/), [2oceansvibe](https://www.2oceansvibe.com/world/south-africa/whatsapp-business-reply-fee-south-africa-august-2026/), [ChatMaxima rate card](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/south-africa/)). Current SA rates: utility about $0.0076 (roughly R0.14), marketing about $0.0379 (roughly R0.68). Theo's R0.15 to R0.40 estimate is on the safe side, which is fine for planning.

### Per-message AI cost

Assumes each Lexi reply sends about 3,000 tokens (her instructions, the price list, the chat so far, open slots) and gets back about 250. A Haiku-class model ($1 in, $5 out per million tokens) costs about **R0.08 a reply**. A Sonnet-class model ($3 in, $15 out) costs about **R0.23 a reply** ([Anthropic Haiku 4.5](https://www.anthropic.com/claude/haiku), [BenchLM pricing](https://benchlm.ai/anthropic/api-pricing)). Prompt caching can cut the input part a lot. I don't know which model the rebuild uses, so both are shown.

### Monthly cost to run one client

| Cost | Solo braider (35 bookings, ~500 Lexi replies) | 3-chair salon (200 bookings, ~2,000 replies) |
|---|---|---|
| Meta replies | R0 (under 1,000 free) | ~R120 (1,000 over x R0.12) |
| Meta reminders and review requests | ~R10 | ~R50 |
| Claude API (Haiku-class to Sonnet-class) | R40 to R115 | R160 to R460 |
| Make.com (about 6 credits a reply; Core is $9 for 10,000) | ~R50 | ~R200 |
| Domain and hosting (CHALES rate) | R109 | R109 |
| Yoco fees on deposits | paid by the owner from deposits, not Theo | same |
| **Cash cost** | **~R210 to R285** | **~R640 to R940** |
| Theo's time after launch | ~1 hr a month | ~2 hrs a month |

One-off time: 4 to 8 hours to build the website, 2 to 3 hours to set up Lexi and test, 1 hour for the visit. Call it a day and a half per client.

What this means:

- A solo client at R999 leaves Theo about R700 a month. At R599, about R300 to R400.
- A busy 3-chair salon at R999 could leave him as little as R60 if Lexi runs on a Sonnet-class model. That's the risk in a flat R1,000 price. Fixes: use a cheaper model for simple turns, cap the founding price at a booking volume (say up to 120 bookings a month, then R1,499), or charge per extra stylist calendar.
- Make.com will get expensive past about 10 clients. Moving Lexi's message loop to a small server later would cut that line to almost nothing, but that's a later problem.

## 5. What makes an offer easy to say yes to here

### What the research says

**Hormozi's value equation.** Value goes up with the dream outcome and how sure they are it'll work, and goes down with how long it takes and how much effort it costs them ([Young and Profiting interview](https://youngandprofiting.com/alex-hormozi-the-value-equation-how-to-make-offers-so-good-people-feel-stupid-saying-no-e199/), [Greg Faxon summary](https://www.gregfaxon.com/blog/100m-offers-summary)). For this market:

- Dream outcome: "My phone stops ruling my life and nobody wastes my Saturday."
- Likelihood: the weakest part. They've never seen AI book anything. CHALES is the proof, so the demo has to be Lexi actually booking on their own phone, live, in front of them.
- Time delay: "live in 4 days" is strong. Say it every time.
- Effort: must be near zero. One visit, forward some photos, done.

Hormozi also says the guarantee should be one you'll actually honour, because a guarantee you wriggle out of kills trust faster than having none ([alexhormozi.wiki](https://alexhormozi.wiki/frameworks/grand-slam-offer-anatomy)). In a community where everyone talks, that matters double.

**How Yoco won small SA businesses.** No monthly fee, no contract, signup in a few clicks, a machine within 2 days, no business bank account needed. Word of mouth did the rest once it got into a community ([Yoco blog](https://www.yoco.com/za/blog/unlocking-sme-growth-south-africa/), [Today Africa](https://todayafrica.co/inside-yocos-journey/), [71point4](https://www.71point4.com/digital-payment-solutions-for-informal-businesses/)). That's the template: nothing upfront, nothing locked in, fast.

**Trust.** SA businesses are primed for scams. TransUnion says SA leads the continent in digital fraud ([Vutivi](https://vutivibusiness.co.za/business/online-shopping-scams-poison-digital-trust-for-legitimate-smmes/)). Anything that asks for money before it's proved itself triggers "is this a scam?". In township markets word of mouth is the most trusted recommendation, and stokvel culture runs on trust and recommendations ([Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/Article/196/168/228947.html), [Matriarch](https://www.matriarch.co.za/blog/a-guide-to-effective-stokvel-marketing-strategies/)). I found no hard data on church networks or voice notes specifically **(unverified, but it matches what Theo already knows about his community)**. What builds trust, in practice:

- Theo in person, from the same community, speaking Afrikaans if they do
- A live demo on the prospect's own phone
- A referral from someone they know (Chante is the obvious one)
- Voice notes, not long typed messages
- No money until it works

**Payment methods.** Over 90% of informal businesses are cash-reliant, and only 4% of informal retail transactions are by card ([Business Report](https://businessreport.co.za/opinion/2025-10-20-designing-a-cashless-future-why-south-africas-informal-economy-cannot-be-an-afterthought/), [SME South Africa](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/cash-payments-still-dominate-informal-trade/)). But these are salon owners with bank accounts who already take EFT deposits, so they'll pay by EFT. Debit orders carry baggage in SA: 40 in every 100 reversed debit orders are fraudulent or non-compliant, and from April 2026 people get 60 days to dispute one ([Netcash](https://netcash.co.za/blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-debit-order-fraud-and-the-latest-pasa-regulations/), [Joburg ETC](https://www.joburgetc.com/business/debit-order-dispute-rule-south-africa-2026/)). PayShap is growing fast, 80% of its payments are under R500, and PayShap Request lets a business send a payment request straight to a customer's banking app ([Stitch](https://stitch.money/blog/real-time-payments-in-south-africa-the-state-of-payshap-in-2026), [Netcash](https://netcash.co.za/blog/driving-adoption-of-instant-payments-how-payshap-is-transforming-south-africas-financial-landscape/)).

**My recommendation for collecting:** a WhatsApp on the 1st with a Yoco payment link and Theo's banking details for EFT or PayShap. No debit order for founding clients, because asking for one before trust exists feels like a trap. Offer PayFast card subscriptions later for anyone who wants it automatic ([PayFast subscriptions](https://payfast.io/features/subscriptions/)). Let them pick the bill date (many solos are flush after the weekend, not on the 1st). Don't take cash, it's hard to track and awkward to chase.

## 6. The founding offer

### Recommended: "The Founding 10"

The pitch in one line: *"I set up Lexi and your website for free. You pay nothing until she's booked your first 20 clients. After that it's R999 a month, and you can stop any time with one WhatsApp."*

**What's included**

- Lexi on WhatsApp: answers prices and questions in English or Afrikaans, day and night, books into a Google Calendar, sends reminders
- A mobile website with prices, photos, directions and a "Book on WhatsApp" button
- Domain and hosting
- Google Business Profile set up or cleaned up, with the WhatsApp booking link
- A QR code poster for the door or mirror, and a WhatsApp status post telling clients how to book
- Theo on WhatsApp for changes (new prices, holidays, new services), done within a day
- New features as they're built (deposits, cancel/reschedule, review requests) at no extra cost

**Price:** R999 a month. Setup R0 (normal price R2,998 for Lexi and website setup). Only 10 spots.

**The guarantee (the 20-booking promise):** You pay nothing until Lexi has booked 20 appointments for you. Theo counts them from the booking sheet and sends the owner the list. If she hasn't booked 20 in the first 60 days, it's switched off, no bill, and they keep the Google Business Profile work. A second, smaller promise Theo controls completely: if Lexi ever double-books you, that month is free.

**Terms**

- Month to month. No contract. Cancel with one WhatsApp before the next bill.
- Price locked at R999 for as long as they stay, even when new clients pay more.
- The domain is registered in their business name, so it's theirs if they leave. The site stays up 30 days after they cancel. They can buy it outright for R1,499 if they want to keep it.
- Founding price covers one calendar and up to about 120 bookings a month. Extra stylist calendars R200 each **(suggestion, to protect Theo on busy salons)**.
- What Theo asks in return: an honest voice note or video testimonial after month one, a Google review for Uncle T Agency, and introductions to 2 other owners if they're happy. Plus permission to track two numbers (bookings Lexi made, no-shows before and after).

**What the client has to do:** 15 minutes with Theo, forward their best photos, tell their clients Lexi is there (Theo writes the message), and answer a few test chats before launch day. That's it.

**Theo's risk:** Roughly 1.5 days of work and R200 to R900 a month in costs until each client hits 20 bookings. For a solo braider on 35 bookings a month, 20 bookings takes 3 to 5 weeks, depending on how many come through Lexi rather than old habits. If 10 sign up and 3 never hit 20, Theo loses about 4 or 5 days of work and maybe R1,500 in running costs. Cheap, compared with 7 paying clients and 7 testimonials.

### Variant A: plain R999 bundle, first month free

Same package, same terms, but the free part is just the first month instead of 20 bookings.

- **Pros:** Easiest to explain. Theo knows exactly when billing starts. No arguing over what counts as a booking.
- **Cons:** It's a trial, not a promise. It doesn't prove anything to the owner, so month-two cancellations are likelier. A slow month (January, say) can make Lexi look useless through no fault of hers.
- **Theo's cost and risk:** one month of running costs (R200 to R900) plus build time per client, whether it works or not.

### Variant B: "Lexi Solo" at R599 a month

For one-chair braiders and home-based nail and lash techs.

- **Includes:** Lexi, reminders, one calendar, and a simple one-page site built from a template (price list, photos, map, WhatsApp button). Same R0 setup, same 20-booking promise, same terms. Deposits added when that feature ships.
- **Pros:** Fits the reality that a lot of these operators clear under R10,000 a month. R599 is less than one braid. It's the price that actually gets "no blink".
- **Cons:** Theo clears about R300 to R400 a month per client. Ten of them is R3,000 to R4,000 a month, with the same support load as bigger clients. The template site has to take under 2 hours or the maths breaks.
- **Theo's cost and risk:** same running costs as the solo column in section 4, less website time.

### Variant C: pay per booking

R25 per booking Lexi makes, capped at R999 a month, nothing if she books nothing.

- **Pros:** Zero risk for the owner and it scales with how busy they are. Easy to explain against Nextapt's R1 a booking ("they charge your client R1 and give you a form, I give you a receptionist").
- **Cons:** Unpredictable income for Theo. Owners start noticing every R25 and some will book clients themselves to avoid the fee. A solo on 35 bookings pays R875 anyway, so it isn't really cheaper than the bundle. Invoicing gets messier.
- **Theo's cost and risk:** same costs, less predictable revenue. Probably best kept as a fallback for a prospect who says no to everything else.

**My pick:** lead with the Founding 10 (20-booking promise) at R999 for salons, and offer Lexi Solo at R599 to one-chair operators straight away rather than waiting for them to object. Keep Variant C in the back pocket.

## 8. Objections and honest answers

**"I can just reply myself."**
You can, and you have been. How many WhatsApps did you answer yesterday? Lexi does the same thing at 11pm and while your hands are in someone's hair, and she never forgets to send the reminder. You still see every chat and can jump in whenever you like.

**"R1,000 is too much."**
Fair, it's real money. One client not pitching for a braid costs you around R550, and Lexi's R999 works out to about R33 a day. You also pay nothing until she's booked 20 clients, so you see the value before you spend a cent. If you work alone, there's a R599 version.

**"My clients won't talk to a robot."**
She introduces herself as your booking assistant, so nobody feels tricked, and then chats like a person, in English or Afrikaans, without making them tap buttons or download an app. Most clients just care that they got a reply at 10pm. The ones who want you can ask for you and she'll pass it on straight away. Honest answer: a few older clients might prefer to phone. They still can.

**"What if it books wrong?"**
She can only offer times that are open in your calendar, so she can't double-book you. If she ever does, that month is free. Anything she's unsure about (a custom price, a complaint) she hands to you instead of guessing. And we test it with your friends before it goes live.

**"I'm not techy."**
You don't have to be. I do the whole setup in one visit, and after that you just carry on working. Want to change a price or take a week off? Send me a voice note and it's done within a day.

**"Is this a scam? What's the catch?"**
Reasonable question in this country. There's no contract, no debit order, no money upfront. You pay after Lexi has already booked 20 clients, and you can stop with one WhatsApp. Chante at CHALES has been using it, speak to her.

**"What if WhatsApp blocks my number?"**
Lexi runs on Meta's official WhatsApp system, not the grey-area tools that get numbers banned. The rules are about spam. Lexi only replies to people who message you first and sends reminders for bookings they made.

**"I already use Fresha / Booksy."**
Keep it if it works for you. The difference: your clients don't need an app or a link, there's no 20% or 30% cut on new clients, and you've got someone local to call. Lexi can sit in front of what you have too (not built yet, would need checking per system).
