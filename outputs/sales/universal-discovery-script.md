# The universal discovery script: learn it by heart, use it on any small business

Written 18 Nov 2026 for Theo. Works for salons, barbers, nail techs, med spas, plumbers, builders, cleaners, groomers, driving schools and mobile detailers. The structure never changes. Only the opener question and the pain probes change by trade.

Built on Alex Hormozi's CLOSER (Clarify, Label, Overview the past), his "only questions and restatements" rule, Chris Voss's labels and "does that sound right?", and The Mom Test (ask about the last time). Full background in `hormozi-style-conversation-script.md` and `salon-conversation-guide.md`.

The probes by trade are guesses to test, not facts. The first 20 chats tell us which are real.

---

## The one thing to remember: H.E.L.P. then Thanks

| Letter | Step | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | Hello | Say who you are, why you're here, that you're not selling. Ask for two minutes. |
| **E** | Explore | Ask what's hardest right now. Ask "why that one?" and "what does it cost you?" |
| **L** | Label | Say it back in their words. End with "does that sound about right?" |
| **P** | Past | "What have you tried? How long? How did it work? What else?" Then say it back again. |
| **T** | Thanks | Thank them. Ask "Who else should I speak to?" and "Can I check back in a few weeks?" |

Everything else is detail. If you only remember HELP, you can run the whole conversation.

## The script, word for word (English)

### H: Hello (15 seconds)

> Hi, I'm Theo, I'm from around here. I'm chatting to [salon owners / plumbers / builders / nail techs] to understand what's hard about running the business. I'm not selling anything today. Got two minutes?

If they say no or "I'm busy": "No problem at all. When's a better time?" or "Thanks anyway, have a good day." Mean it.

### E: Explore (60 to 90 seconds)

> What's the hardest part of running the business right now?

Wait. Then, always:
> Why is that the hardest one?
> How long has it been like that?
> What does it cost you, roughly? Time, money, customers?

If they go quiet, use the trade opener from the table further down.

### L: Label (20 seconds)

> So what I'm hearing is [their problem, in their own words], and it's costing you [what they said]. Does that sound about right?

Use their words, not yours. If they say "customers go quiet", you say "customers go quiet". If they correct you, say "got it" and say it again correctly.

### P: Past (60 to 90 seconds)

> What have you tried so far to sort that out?
> How long did you do that for?
> How did that work out?
> What else have you tried?
> Why do you think it didn't stick?

Then say it back:
> So you tried [A] and [B], and the part that's still not solved is [C]. Is that fair?

### Two harder questions (only if they're already open, one at a time)

> If nothing changes, what does the next six months look like?
> Why does it matter to fix it now, and not later?

### T: Thanks (30 seconds)

If they're clearly in pain and curious:
> Thanks for being so open. I build something for exactly this, but I'm only here to learn today. Would it be useful if I showed you something short another time?

If not:
> Thanks for your time, I really appreciate it.

Always:
> Who else should I speak to?
> Can I check back in a few weeks to see how it's going?

## The trade swap-ins

The opener and the probes are the only things that change. Pick one opener to start and two or three probes to dig.

| Trade | Opener question (if "hardest part" gets a blank) | Probes to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| **Hair salon** | "How do you handle your phone when you're mid-client?" | Missed WhatsApps, the same questions all day, no-shows, back-and-forth to agree a time, no time off |
| **Barber** | "How many walk-ins versus bookings, and what happens when it's both?" | Walk-ins blocking bookings, no-shows, WhatsApp while cutting, late arrivals |
| **Nail tech** | "How do clients book, and what goes wrong?" | No-shows, late clients, inspo pictures in DMs, deposits, fill-in reminders |
| **Med spa** | "What happens when someone enquires about a treatment?" | Slow reply to enquiries, no-shows on consultations, the same price and aftercare questions, forms and consent chasing |
| **Plumber** | "What happens to the calls when you're under a sink?" | Missed calls become lost jobs, after-hours emergencies, quotes that take days, chasing payment, no-shows at the customer's home |
| **Builder / construction** | "How do you keep up with enquiries while you're on site?" | Slow quotes, customers asking for updates, chasing deposits and payments, scope changes, unanswered calls |
| **Cleaner** | "How do new customers get a quote from you?" | Back-and-forth on quotes, cancellations, recurring bookings, no-access days |
| **Dog groomer** | "What do people ask before they book?" | Breed and size price questions, no-shows, late drop-offs |
| **Driving school** | "How do you handle lesson changes?" | Reschedules, learner no-shows, instructor diary clashes |
| **Mobile detailer** | "How do you quote and book a job?" | Photo quotes, location and travel questions, weather reschedules, no-shows |

Questions that work in any trade:
- "When was the last time that happened?"
- "What did it cost you?"
- "What do you do about it now?"
- "Have you tried to fix it? What happened?"
- "Where do you keep your bookings or jobs?"

## Only if they ask "how does it work?" (under 3 minutes, one story)

Tell a short true story about the result, not the system. No Make, Claude or calendars.

> I built a WhatsApp assistant for a salon in Bellville. When a client messages, it answers, finds a free slot, books it and sends a reminder the day before. The owner stays with the person in the chair. Does that sound like something that would help, or is it not the real problem?

Then stop talking and let them answer.

Swap the trade when you're speaking to a plumber or builder ("so a customer messages while you're on a roof, and they get an answer and a time straight away"). Only say what Lexi can really do today. Lexi takes day and time, checks the calendar, confirms, and sends reminders. It doesn't send quotes or collect photos.

## Memorise it in layers (about a week)

1. **Day 1:** Learn only H.E.L.P.T. Say the five letters out loud and what each one means, 5 times.
2. **Day 2:** Learn the Hello line until you can say it without looking. Say it in the mirror 10 times.
3. **Day 3:** Learn Explore and Label. Say them with the three follow-up questions.
4. **Day 4:** Learn the Past questions in order. Practise the "so you tried A and B" restatement with made-up answers.
5. **Day 5:** Learn Thanks, and the two harder questions.
6. **Day 6:** Run the whole script out loud with a friend or Nita playing the owner. Ask them to be difficult once: "I'm too busy", "I don't need anything".
7. **Day 7:** Record yourself on a voice note and listen back. Cut every "um" and long sentence.

Each day, run the whole thing once through before you add the new piece.

**The pocket card.** Write this on one card or in your phone notes, to look at in the car before you go in:

```
H  Hi, I'm Theo, from around here. Chatting to owners about what's hard. Not selling. Two minutes?
E  Hardest part right now? Why that one? What does it cost?
L  So what I'm hearing is ___, costing you ___. Sound right?
P  What have you tried? How long? How did it work? What else?
T  Thanks. Who else should I speak to? Can I check back in a few weeks?
```

## How to sound like you mean it

- Hormozi says it plainly: "If you actually give a [care], you will know what questions to ask in what order." Learn the order, not the performance.
- Slow down. Talk at about two-thirds of your normal speed. Pauses sound confident.
- Say less than you think you should. After they answer, count to two before you speak.
- If you lose your place, say "Hang on, let me make sure I've got that right" and restate. That sounds like care, not a fumble.
- If you're nervous, ask a question. Never fill the gap with a pitch.
- Don't pretend. If you're here to learn, say so. If they ask what you do, say "I build WhatsApp booking tools, but today I'm here to learn how the work really goes."
