# Salon conversation guide: find the pain before we sell anything

Written 17 Nov 2026 for Theo. Goal: 20 casual chats with salon owners. No pitch, no Lexi. Listen, then write down their exact words.

The 15 frustrations below are guesses built from our own evidence (the salons' auto-replies, the competitor review research, Google reviews, what Chales and Lexi show). They are things to test, not facts. Your 20 chats tell us which ones are real.

## How to open (30 seconds)

> Hi, I'm Theo, I'm from around here. I'm chatting to salon owners to understand what's hard about running one. I'm not selling anything today. Got two minutes?

If they ask what you do: "I build WhatsApp booking tools, but today I'm just here to learn." Then go straight back to a question.

## The rules (from The Mom Test)

1. Ask about the last time it happened, not what they'd do. "Tell me about the last time a client no-showed" gets a story. "Would you use a booking tool?" gets a polite lie.
2. Don't mention Lexi, price or features. If they ask, answer in one line and ask another question.
3. Let silence sit. The second answer is usually the real one.
4. Mirror the last 2 to 3 words ("Every weekend?") and wait.
5. Ask "How much does that cost you?" or "What do you do about it now?" for each problem. A pain they already spend time or money on is a real one.
6. End with two asks: "Who else should I speak to?" and "Can I check back in a few weeks?"
7. Write their exact words down straight after, in your phone, before the next chat.

## The 15 frustrations, with a casual way in

| # | Frustration (our guess) | Casual opener | Listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Messages and calls go unanswered while hands are busy | "How do you handle your phone when you're mid-client?" | Missed messages, calling back at night, a client lost |
| 2 | The same questions all day (prices, hours, parking, location) | "What do clients ask you the most? Do you get sick of answering?" | A list they type out again and again |
| 3 | No-shows and last-minute cancellations | "Tell me about the last time someone didn't pitch up." | How often, how much it cost, whether they charge |
| 4 | Back-and-forth to agree a time | "When a new client messages, how many messages does it take to book?" | Long chats, clients who vanish halfway |
| 5 | Clients running late, everyone else waiting | "What happens when someone arrives late?" | Knock-on delays, angry next client |
| 6 | Price arguments and quote mix-ups | "Do clients ever argue about the price after?" | Quotes over WhatsApp that changed, "last time it was cheaper" |
| 7 | Chasing deposits and proof of payment | "How do you handle deposits? Does anyone ever not pay?" | Screenshots of payment, unpaid holds |
| 8 | Long consultations by message before a booking | "How do clients explain what they want, over WhatsApp or in the chair?" | Inspo pictures, vague asks, wasted time |
| 9 | No time off: answering clients at night and on Sundays | "When do you actually switch off from clients?" | Replying in bed, on days off, weekends |
| 10 | Empty gaps and quiet days | "When are you quiet, and what do you do about it?" | Dead mornings, slots that don't fill |
| 11 | Regulars who forget to rebook | "How do your regulars rebook? Do any drift away?" | Clients who go six months and disappear |
| 12 | Booking apps that annoy (fees, commission, clients won't use the link) | "Have you ever tried a booking app? What happened?" | Fees, "my clients still WhatsApp me", gave up |
| 13 | Who's booked with whom (staff, chair rental) | "How do you keep track when more than one person is working?" | Double bookings, mix-ups between stylists |
| 14 | Remembering each client's history (colour, allergies, patch tests) | "How do you remember what each client had last time?" | Notebooks, memory, WhatsApp scroll-back |
| 15 | The diary lives in their head, on paper or in WhatsApp scroll | "Where do you keep your bookings?" | Paper diary, phone, no single place |

Do not read this as a script. Pick the three that fit the salon in front of you.

## Questions that dig deeper (use after any of the above)

- "When was the last time?"
- "What did that cost you, roughly?"
- "What do you do about it now?"
- "Have you tried to fix it? What happened?"
- "Why is that the worst one?"
- "If you could wave a wand, what would go away first?" (only once, near the end)

## What to write down after each chat (one line each)

Salon, owner's first name, date, which frustrations came up (by number), their exact words for the top one, what they do about it now, any rand figure or hours per week, and whether they'd be open to a follow-up.

## After 20 chats

1. Count which frustration numbers came up most. The top three become the headline on the website, the Facebook posts and the first line of the follow-up messages.
2. Keep every exact quote. Owners' own words beat anything we write.
3. Anyone who said "I'm going through that" with a rand figure or hours attached is the first person to speak to about Lexi, later and gently.
4. If the guesses above are wrong, we change the website and messages to match what they said.

## Good places to find them

- Walk in on a quiet weekday (Tuesday to Thursday, mid-morning). Avoid Fridays and Saturdays.
- The salons already messaged: they already know your name, which makes the chat easy. Say "I messaged you a few weeks back, I'd rather just hear how it really is for you."
- Facebook groups for salon owners: ask the questions as plain posts ("What's your biggest daily headache running a salon?"). Check the group rules first.
