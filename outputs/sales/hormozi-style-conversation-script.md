# A script for the 20 salon conversations (built on Alex Hormozi's CLOSER and his "three things to say")

Written 17 Nov 2026 for Theo. Read alongside `salon-conversation-guide.md` (the 15 frustrations to test).

## What this is built from

Two of Hormozi's own videos, pulled with Supadata on 17 Nov 2026:
- "I Taught 116+ Salesmen My Closing Framework" (the CLOSER framework: Clarify, Label, Overview the past pain, Sell the vacation, Explain away concerns, Reinforce).
- "3 Simple Steps To Close Any Sale" (the only three things a seller should say: questions, restatements, short stories).

We use only the first half. Clarify, Label and Overview are exactly what you want for a discovery chat. Selling, explaining away concerns and reinforcing come later, after an owner has told you the pain and asked about a fix. These chats are for learning, so the product doesn't come up unless they ask.

## Hormozi's rules, in his words, and what they mean for you

1. **Only three things come out of your mouth: questions, restatements, and short stories.** For these chats, drop the stories. Questions and restatements only.
2. **Don't teach.** He says people won't listen until they buy, and "technobabble" is the fastest way to lose them. Don't explain WhatsApp tools or what you build.
3. **Restate what you heard, then ask "does that sound about right?"** The owner has to say the problem out loud, in their own words. "I can't cure cancer until you admit that you have it."
4. **Run the pain cycle.** What have you tried? How long for? How did it work? What else? You're finished when there's nothing left they've tried.
5. **Shut up when they're talking.** He says the reason a person buys is how understood they feel.
6. **Sincerity can't be scripted, and he says so.** His line: "If you actually give a [care], you will know what questions to ask in what order." Scripts exist to copy a conversation between someone who cares and someone they can help. So learn the order, not the words, and ask only what you're genuinely curious about.
7. **Don't pitch until they're ready.** He says to stay in the questions until the person asks how it works, or at least gives you permission.

## The script, step by step

### 0. Open (permission and honesty)

> Hi, I'm Theo, I'm from around here. I'm chatting to salon owners to understand what's hard about running one. I'm not selling anything today. Got two minutes?

### 1. Clarify (why this, why now)

Pick the opener that fits and then ask "why":
- "What's the hardest part of running the salon right now?"
- "What takes up more of your day than it should?"
- "What would you change first if you could?"

Follow-ups, always:
- "Why is that the hardest one?"
- "How long has it been like that?"
- "What does it cost you, roughly? Time, money, clients?"

### 2. Label (restate it back, get the yes)

> So what I'm hearing is [their problem, in their words], and it's costing you [what they said]. Does that sound about right?

If they say "yes, exactly", you've got a real pain. If they correct you, good, restate again.

### 3. Overview the past (the pain cycle)

Ask these in order, as many times as there's more to hear:
- "What have you tried so far to sort that out?"
- "How long did you do that for?"
- "How did that work out?"
- "What else have you tried?"
- "Why do you think it didn't stick?"

Then restate, and mean it:
> So you tried [A] and [B], and the part that still isn't solved is [C]. Is that fair?

### 4. Two harder questions (Hormozi: "closers ask hard questions")

Use them gently, one at a time, and only when the owner is already open:
- "If nothing changes, what does the next six months look like?"
- "Why is it important to fix that now and not later?"

### 5. Close the chat, don't close a sale

If they're clearly in pain and curious:
> Thanks for being so open. I build something for exactly this, but I'm only here to learn today. Would it be useful if I showed you something short another time?

If they say yes, that is permission. Book a time. If they say no, you say thanks and mean it.

Always, to everyone:
- "Who else should I speak to?"
- "Can I check back in a few weeks to see how it's going?"

## Only if they ask "how does it work?" (the story, under three minutes)

Hormozi's rule: tell a short story that shows the result, not the system. No talk of Make, Claude, calendars or modules. Two true stories you can use:

**Story 1: Chales.** "I built a WhatsApp assistant for a salon in Bellville called CHALES Hair Boutique. When a client messages, the assistant answers, finds a free slot and books it, and sends a reminder the day before. The owner stays with the person in the chair." Only add numbers you can prove (Chales has about 100 bookings on the sheet, but check before quoting).

**Story 2: the receptionist.** "Think of a salon with a good receptionist who never goes home, never takes a lunch, and answers every message straight away. That's what the assistant is. Do you think that would change your evenings?"

After the story, ask: "Does that sound like something that would help, or is it not the real problem?" Then stop and let them answer.

## Things to avoid (Hormozi's own mistakes, which he says cost him sales)

- Telling them what's wrong with their pricing or process. He tried this at Gym Launch, people took notes and never bought.
- Talking for five minutes straight. If your side runs longer than two or three sentences, ask a question.
- Pitching because you're nervous. If you feel the urge, ask "what else?" instead.

## How to sound sincere (practical)

- Memorise the five stages, not the sentences. If it comes out in your own words, in English or Afrikaans, it sounds like you.
- Be honest. If you only came to listen, say so. If someone asks "are you selling?", answer: "I do build WhatsApp booking tools, but today I'm only here to learn how the work really goes."
- Write something down in front of them. It shows you care.
- Say "tell me more" and mean it.
- Practise out loud twice with someone (a friend or Nita), who plays the owner and pushes back.
