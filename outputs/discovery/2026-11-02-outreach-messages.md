# Outreach messages that double as research

Rule: these ask one real question and offer nothing. If they reply, listen first. Mention Lexi only if they ask what you do, in one line, then ask another question. Never send the same text to two people in the same salon.

Send from Theo's own number, not Lexi's. Send mid-week mornings. One message, no follow-up for 4 days.

## 1. Cold, to a salon or stylist (English)
Hi, Theo here. I'm from Cape Town and my daughter runs a hair salon, Chales Hair Boutique. I'm trying to understand what's really hard about running a salon day to day. Can I ask you one quick question? What's the most frustrating part of your week?

## 2. Cold (Afrikaans)
Hi, dis Theo. My dogter het 'n haarsalon, Chales Hair Boutique, en ek probeer verstaan wat regtig moeilik is aan 'n salon bestuur. Mag ek jou een vinnige vraag vra? Wat is die frustrerendste deel van jou week?

## 3. Cold, for nail techs, lash and brow, home-based
Hi, Theo here, Cape Town. I'm talking to people who work for themselves in beauty about what makes the work hard, not the hair or nails part, the running-it part. What's the one thing you wish was easier?

## 4. Warm leads who already replied (Zanzibar, Sistergirl, Reeva, JEM, Jason)
Hi [name], thanks again for getting back to me. Before I tell you anything about what I do, can I ask you something? When did a client last not pitch, or a booking get lost on WhatsApp, and what did that cost you that day?

## 5. In person, said out loud
"Hi, I'm Theo. My daughter has a salon over in [area]. I'm trying to learn what makes the business side hard. Can I buy you a coffee, or do a quick chat while you're on a break?"

## Follow-up questions after they reply (pick one, never a list)
- Tell me about the last time that happened.
- What did it cost you, roughly?
- What have you tried to fix it?
- Why did you stop?
- If that was sorted tomorrow, what would change for you?

## If they ask "so what do you do?"
"I build WhatsApp tools for salons. But I'm not selling today, I want to understand the problem first. What happened with [what they just said]?"

## Do not
- Do not say "AI", "automation" or "chatbot" in the first message.
- Do not offer a price, a demo or a free trial in the first conversation.
- Do not pretend to be a student or a survey company. You are a local person who builds tools and wants to understand first. That is true and it is enough.
- Do not argue with an answer. Write it down in their words.

## Track every reply
Log in the problem log: date, name, business type, area, channel, their exact words, cost in rand or hours, what they tried. Reply rate matters too: if fewer than 1 in 5 answer, change the message, not the audience.

## Send plan
Week 1: 20 messages (10 English, 10 Afrikaans) across Bellville, Mitchell's Plain, Parow, Goodwood, plus the five warm leads. Target 5 real conversations. Review the replies, then adjust the wording.
