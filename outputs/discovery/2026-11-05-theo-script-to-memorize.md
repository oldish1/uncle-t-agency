# Theo's script: learn it until you do not have to think

Use it in person, on the phone and in voice notes. Same shape every time, so it becomes automatic. Say it in your own words once you know it. Do not read it out. The shape is what you memorize.

## The shape: L.A.S.T.
**L**isten first. **A**sk about the last time. **S**how the number. **T**ake one next step.
Never skip a letter. Never go to S until they have said a pain and a cost.

---

## 1. Open (15 seconds)
English:
"Hi, I'm Theo. My daughter has a salon, Chales Hair Boutique, in [area]. I'm trying to understand what's really hard about running a salon. Got two minutes?"

Afrikaans:
"Hi, ek is Theo. My dogter het 'n salon, Chales Hair Boutique, in [area]. Ek probeer verstaan wat regtig moeilik is aan 'n salon bestuur. Het jy twee minute?"

## 2. Ask about the LAST time (the three questions, in order)
1. "Tell me about the last time a booking went wrong. What happened?"
2. "What did that cost you? Roughly, in rand?"
3. "What have you tried to fix it?"

Then stop talking. Count to five in your head. Their next sentence is the real answer.

Follow-ups, one at a time, never a list:
- "How often does that happen, a week?"
- "Who answers WhatsApp when you're busy with a client?"
- "What happens at night?"

## 3. Bridge (only after they gave you a pain and a number)
"So if I've got this right: [their words]. About [number] times a month, at about R[amount] each. That's roughly R[total] a month. Is that fair?"

If they say yes, you earned the next part. If they say no, ask what is wrong with the number and fix it with them.

## 4. The 20-second pitch (problem first)
"That's what I fix. I built a WhatsApp assistant, Lexi. When a client messages, it books them, reminds them the day before, and answers at night while you're working. At my daughter's salon it answers in a few seconds. I'd like to show you. It takes one minute."

Afrikaans:
"Dis presies wat ek regmaak. Ek het 'n WhatsApp-assistent gebou, Lexi. Wanneer 'n kliënt boodskappe stuur, maak sy die bespreking, herinner hulle die dag vantevore en antwoord in die aand terwyl jy werk. By my dogter se salon antwoord sy binne sekondes. Mag ek jou wys? Dit vat 'n minuut."

## 5. Show, do not tell (one minute)
Hand them your phone or ask for theirs. "Send a voice note to this number, say you want a booking on Saturday, and see what happens." (Demo number: 076 492 4196.)
Say nothing while it replies. Let them see it.

## 6. Take ONE next step
Pick one:
- "Can I send you the number to try on your own phone tonight?" (WhatsApp number)
- "Can I come back Thursday and set it up on your number for a week, so you see it with your own clients?"
- "Who else should I show this to?"

Never "think about it". Always a day and a time.

---

## Price, only when they ask
"It's R1,499 to set up, then R499 a month. That's [R499 divided by what one lost slot costs them] lost appointments a month to pay for it. Everything is included, and I set it up myself."

If they ask how it compares to a R79 app: "Those are good apps. You set them up yourself, and the WhatsApp side has extra costs. I come to you, set it up, and fix it when it breaks."

## The five objections (one line each, then a question)
1. **"Too expensive."** "Fair. What does one empty slot cost you? How many a month? Then we see if it's worth it."
2. **"I'm fine, I use a diary / my phone."** "A diary works until a Saturday goes quiet. What happened last time someone didn't pitch?"
3. **"I'm not good with technology."** "That's why I do it for you. You do not touch anything. If you can use WhatsApp you can use this."
4. **"I don't trust it with my clients."** "Good, you shouldn't blindly. That's why you try it for a week on your own number first. Chanté's clients use it every day."
5. **"Let me think about it."** "Of course. What would you want to be sure about? I'll answer it now so you're not left guessing."

## Three rules
1. Silence is a tool. After a question, shut up.
2. No price before a pain and a number.
3. Every conversation ends with a day and a time, or an honest "not for me, thank you", which is also useful.

---

## The memory card (one line each, on your phone)
1. Open: who I am, my daughter's salon, two minutes.
2. Last time? Cost? Tried?
3. Bridge: your words, your number.
4. Pitch: Lexi, one sentence, the result.
5. Show: voice note, say nothing.
6. Next step: a day and a time.

---

## How to learn it in 14 days (from the reel: memorize, role-play, feedback, review)
- **Days 1 to 3, memorize.** Say each section out loud 10 times a day while driving or at work. Record yourself on your phone and play it back. Aim to say the whole thing without looking.
- **Days 4 to 7, role-play.** Chanté, Nita or a friend plays a salon owner. Give them the five objections. Do three full run-throughs a day, ten minutes each. They tell you where you rambled or rushed.
- **Days 5 to 14, real conversations.** Voice-note yourself right after each one. Note where you froze. Fix one thing a day.
- **Day 7 and day 14, review.** Listen back to three recordings. Ask: did I listen first? Did I get a number? Did I take one next step?
- **Shadow.** Ask Chanté if you can sit near the desk for an hour on a quiet day and listen to how her clients actually talk.
- **Game tape.** Each week listen to your own voice notes. Keep what works and drop what doesn't.

## Scoring yourself
After each conversation give yourself a point for each: listened first, got a pain, got a rand number, did the bridge, showed the demo, took a next step. Six out of six is a great conversation, whatever they decided. Log it in the scorecard.
