# Why the cheap tools are cheap, and where the holes are

Date: 2 Nov 2026. Sources: Booklink, Blyssbook and Chatty pricing and docs pages, Capterra and Software Advice reviews of Booklink, Chatty's own comparison page. Not checked: Nextapt, WAFlowBot, BizAI (our named competitors), Fresha/Booksy in depth. No Hello Peter or Google reviews found for any of them (Apify limit hit).

## Plain answer: why are they cheap?
They sell software, not service. One product, thousands of customers, the owner sets it up and runs it. Support is by email. That is a real business model, not a trick. Booklink's reviews are mostly 4.6 out of 5 with praise for fast email support and quick feature requests. So "they must be doing it wrong" is mostly not true. Saying it is would not survive the first owner who uses one.

## The holes (all from their own pages or reviews)

1. **The owner does all the work.**
   - Chatty's setup list: a brand-new SIM or eSIM that has never had WhatsApp, a Facebook login, about 25 minutes, and a card for WhatsApp's message charges.
   - Blyssbook: sign in with Facebook through a Meta pop-up and choose or create a WhatsApp Business account. A new number may need a verification code in WhatsApp Manager.
   - Our own ledger: Meta verification took us weeks and several failed attempts, as people who do this for a living. A salon owner will not finish it.
2. **A new number means moving the clients.** Chatty needs a number used only for bookings. Clients have to learn the new number. Lexi works on the salon's own number.
3. **WhatsApp message fees are extra and the owner pays Meta directly.** Chatty says so on its pricing page. Booklink's reviewer: "WhatsApp notifications require credit and eventually cost extra after free credit is used up" (50 free a month on Pro).
4. **The real price is not the headline price.**
   - Booklink Free is 1 service, 1 team member, 30 bookings a month. Reminders, payments and calendar sync need Pro (R79).
   - Blyssbook's R184 tier has no automatic WhatsApp reminders. Reminders and the AI reply bot start on Professional (about R351 a month). The plan prices are in dollars, so the rand price moves.
   - Chatty shows no price on its pricing page ("priced at signup").
   - Starter plans are capped at 30 to 50 bookings a month.
5. **Settings that suit someone else.** Blyssbook ships message templates in English, Arabic and Spanish. No Afrikaans, no isiXhosa. Chatty's own copy is English.
6. **Newness.** Blyssbook states it had too few live salons (under 10) to publish how well its bot works. Its WhatsApp bot, the Instagram and Messenger bots and the AI phone receptionist are labelled beta or rolling out.
7. **Support is email-based.** Booklink replies fast by email (good). None of them offer to visit, call or fix it with you in person.
8. **Gaps one Booklink reviewer named.** Limited booking page styling, only Google Calendar, and one three-month user said the paid version left them doing "heavy lifting" on payments and the booking didn't land in their diary unless payment went through.
9. **They don't fill the salon.** None of them sells getting new clients. They help the clients you already have.
10. **Blyssbook's bot pauses 12 hours when staff reply.** Fine, but it means the owner is still in the chat.

## What cheap really costs the owner (for the interviews)
- Time: the setup, the first month of fixing, the weekly checking.
- Mistakes: a wrong setting means missed bookings with nobody to call.
- Message fees to Meta on top of the plan.
- The upgrade: the feature they want sits on the next tier up.
- Switching: once their clients are on it, leaving is hard.
Test all of these on real owners. Ask: "Have you tried a booking app? What happened?" If most say "I never got round to it", that is the hole. If many say "I use it and it's fine", then the cheap tools win that owner.

## The Hormozi question: sell to people with money
The argument for it is arithmetic.
- Our price: R499 a month (R5,988 a year) plus R1,499 setup.
- A typical Cape Flats salon service is R150 to R450. A no-show costs one service. We have to save about 2 to 4 appointments a month to pay for ourselves, and the owner has to believe it.
- A med spa or dental practice loses R800 to R5,000 per empty slot. Saving one slot pays for the whole month at a much higher price.
- Cheap-tool buyers compare us with R79. Higher-ticket owners compare us with a receptionist's salary.

The counter-argument, honestly:
- Higher-ticket owners already have software, a receptionist or an agency, so the sales cycle is longer and they expect proof and case studies. We have one flagship client and no paying customer.
- Our edge (local, Afrikaans, in person, trust) matters most to the small owners we know. It matters less to a clinic.
- Moving up too early could mean selling to people we do not understand. The interviews and the CHALES data are our evidence today.

## Recommendation (to test, not to assume)
1. Keep salons as the proof engine. Do not drop them.
2. In every interview, write down the price of one lost appointment and what the owner pays for software and staff now.
3. Add a second pass: 10 conversations with owners of higher-ticket local businesses (med spas, aesthetics clinics, dental, driving schools, dog groomers, installers) using the same research-first messages.
4. After both, compare: pain named, rand per lost slot, willingness to pay. Pick the segment where all three are highest.
5. Price an offer for that segment on value, with a quantified quick win and a guarantee, not on a rate card.
