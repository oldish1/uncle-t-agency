# Client problem discovery: start from the box

Status: draft for Theo's review, 2 Nov 2026. Nothing here has been run yet.

## Why we're doing this

We built the solution first. Lexi is fast, stable and proven on one salon. But after two months we have zero paying clients, and the one live client (CHALES) is slow. Lexi answers "how do I take bookings?" Nobody has yet told us that bookings are their biggest problem.

Evidence that the problem may be somewhere else: the best-performing thing we found at CHALES was not booking at all. Her TikTok (4,250 followers) was sending leads to the wrong WhatsApp number. That is a demand and follow-up problem, and Lexi can't fix a customer who never reaches her.

The video's point that applies: a role is a list of bullet points, and you can't hand off a job you haven't done yourself. We can't sell the fix until we can write the problem in bullet points in the client's own words.

## Thinking inside the box

The box is what we already have: Lexi, a flagship client, a working demo, five warm leads, a lead scraper, and Theo's standing in the community. Inside it, the cheapest way to learn is:

1. **Use the five warm leads as interviews, not pitches.** Zanzibar, Sistergirl, Reeva, JEM and Jason already replied. They cost nothing to talk to.
2. **Use CHALES as the lab.** Chanté's WhatsApp, calendar and sheet are real data. Read it before asking anyone anything.
3. **Do the job ourselves first.** Spend one week doing what a receptionist does for CHALES by hand, and log every time it hurts.

## Step 1: Learn from the data we already have (this week, no new outreach)

From CHALES' chat history and booking sheet, answer:

- What do customers actually message about? Count by type: booking, price, "are you open", directions, membership, complaints.
- How many messages arrive outside opening hours? How many got a reply late?
- How many bookings are no-shows or cancels? What happens to the empty slot?
- Where do new customers come from? TikTok, word of mouth, walk-ins, Instagram?
- How many leads never became bookings, and where did they drop off?

Output: a one-page "what really happens at CHALES" with numbers.

## Step 2: Interview 10 owners (weeks 1 to 2)

Five warm leads first, then five from the lead scraper list. Short, in person or by voice note. The rule: they talk, we listen, we do not mention Lexi until the end.

Questions (adapt to Afrikaans or English as they prefer):

1. Walk me through the last time a customer booked with you. What happened, step by step?
2. What was the last booking you lost? What happened?
3. What's the most annoying part of your week that has nothing to do with doing hair?
4. How many customers do you have, and how many are regulars? What brings new ones?
5. What have you tried to fix this already? What did it cost? Why did you stop?
6. If one thing about your business got fixed by Friday, what would it be?
7. What do you pay for every month right now (data, airtime, apps, rent)? Where does R499 sit next to that?

Log every answer in `outputs/discovery/problem-log.md` (or a Google Sheet): owner, business type, their exact words, what it costs them in rand or hours, what they tried.

## Step 3: Find the pattern (end of week 2)

- Cluster the quotes. Which problem do 3 or more owners name unprompted?
- Rank by pain (rand or hours lost a month) and by frequency.
- Write the winner as one sentence in their words, not ours.
- Check: does Lexi solve it? If yes, the pitch becomes their sentence. If no, we've saved months. If it's partly demand (getting customers), the offer changes.

## Step 4: Pick one quick win and put a number on it

Per Nate: one fast win, quantified. Example shapes, to be chosen from what the interviews say:

- "Save you X hours a week on WhatsApp"
- "Fill Y empty slots a month"
- "Bring back Z lapsed customers"

Run it for one warm lead at a discount, track the number, get the testimonial. This replaces "we have a system" with "we fixed this one thing, here's the proof."

## Step 5: Keep it adaptable

- A **problem log** that never closes. Every client conversation adds a row.
- A **monthly review** of the log: has the top problem changed? Does the offer still match?
- Every client request we fix twice becomes a Lexi feature, not a one-off.
- Each automation we sell has one human owner (Theo) and a "last checked" date. Steps 3 and 4 from the video, applied to our own clients.

## What success looks like

By 16 Nov: 10 interviews logged, one problem sentence in clients' own words, and a decision on whether the offer stays, changes or pivots.
By 30 Nov: one paying founding client on a quantified quick win.
