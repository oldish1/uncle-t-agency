# The sales masters, boiled down for Uncle T

> Researched 19 Nov 2026 (Firecrawl, book summaries, the gurus' own sites and videos). This is the brain the **sales-coach** skill teaches from. Every framework below ends with how it applies to selling Lexi (R1,499 once-off + R499/month) to a Cape Town salon owner, because a lesson you can't use on Monday is just entertainment.

---

## The honest bit first

You asked for the ruthless 1% car salesman too. Here's what the research says: the high-pressure dealership tricks (fake "manager approval", the four-square payment sheet, never letting someone leave) work when you'll never see that buyer again. You live in the same community as your clients. One auntie in Mitchell's Plain telling her church group "that guy pushed me" costs you ten deals. Your edge is trust, so take the *discipline* of the top car guys (follow-up, referrals, handling objections without flinching) and leave the tricks.

Jordan Belfort is in here because his Straight Line system is genuinely good at structure and tone. He also went to prison for fraud. Take the method, not the morals.

## 1. Alex Hormozi: the offer and the close

**The Value Equation** (from *$100M Offers*). How much someone values your offer:

> (Dream outcome × How sure they are it'll work) ÷ (How long it takes × How much effort it costs them)

Push the top up, push the bottom down. For Lexi:
- Dream outcome: "Your book fills itself while you're doing hair. No more answering WhatsApp at 11pm."
- Likelihood: show Chales running live on your phone. Proof beats promises every time.
- Time delay: "Live in 7 days."
- Effort: "You do nothing. I set it up, I keep it running, R499 a month and it's my problem."

**Grand Slam Offer.** Make the offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Stack bonuses (free Google Business Profile setup, review requests, a done-for-you price list), add a guarantee ("If Lexi doesn't book you one extra client in the first month, I refund the R1,499"), add scarcity that's real ("I only onboard 2 salons a month because I set each one up by hand").

**C.L.O.S.E.R.** His six-step sales conversation:
1. **Clarify** why they're here. "What made you want to chat today?"
2. **Label** the problem out loud. "So you're losing bookings because you can't answer WhatsApp while your hands are in someone's hair."
3. **Overview** past pain. "What have you tried? How did that go?" (Let them tell you the paper diary failed.)
4. **Sell the vacation**, not the plane ride. Talk about the result (full book, Sundays off), not how Make.com works.
5. **Explain away concerns.** Objections handled one at a time.
6. **Reinforce** the decision after they say yes. "You're going to love Monday morning when you see what came in over the weekend."

**The Core Four** (from *$100M Leads*): warm outreach, cold outreach, posting content, paid ads. You start with warm outreach (people who already know you) because it's free and it converts best. **Rule of 100:** 100 outreach actions a day, every day, for 100 days. For you, with a day job, a realistic version is 20 a day, every day.

## 2. Gary Vaynerchuk: attention before the ask

**Jab, jab, jab, right hook.** Jabs are free value (tips, before/afters, entertainment). The right hook is the ask ("Book now on WhatsApp"). Most small businesses only throw right hooks, which is why nobody watches. Chales's TikTok data already proves this: offer posts with a price perform, but they work because the demo posts earned the attention first.

**Document, don't create.** Don't invent content. Film what's already happening: the wash, the blow-dry, the client's face at the mirror. Chanté does 20 transformations a week. That's 20 reels she's not posting.

**The $1.80 strategy.** Leave your "two cents" (a real comment, not "nice 🔥") on 90 posts a day from local people and businesses. That's how a dead account gets noticed for free. For Chales: comment on Bellville and Durbanville moms, local event pages, brides-to-be.

**Day-trade attention.** Go where people's eyes are now and cheap. In Cape Town in 2026 that's TikTok and WhatsApp Status, then Instagram Reels.

## 3. Tony Robbins: your state sells

**State before strategy.** Your energy walking into a salon decides the sale before you open your mouth. Two minutes before every pitch: shoulders back, breathe, remember your best win. Sounds soft. The top closers all do some version of it.

**The 6 human needs:** certainty, variety, significance, connection, growth, contribution. Every buyer is chasing a few of these. A salon owner wants **certainty** (bills paid), **significance** (being *the* salon in the area), and **connection** (clients who come back). Sell to the need, not the feature.

**Rapport by matching.** Match their pace, volume and language. If the owner speaks Afrikaans, you switch. That's your unfair advantage over Telecloud, and it's pure Robbins.

## 4. Joe Girard: the car salesman who did it clean

Guinness World Records named Joe Girard the world's greatest retail salesman. 13,001 cars sold one at a time at a Chevrolet dealership in Detroit, 1963 to 1977. No fleet deals. And he did it on relationships, not pressure.

**The Law of 250.** Every person knows about 250 people who'll show up at their wedding or funeral. Treat one customer badly and you lose 250. Treat one well and you gain access to 250. This is the Cape Flats in one sentence.

**He never let a customer forget him.** He sent every customer a card every single month that said "I like you." Your version: a WhatsApp voice note to every client and lead on their birthday and once a month with something useful.

**Bird dogs.** He paid people a referral fee for every buyer they sent. Your version: R200 off the next month for any client who refers a salon that signs up. Chanté is your first bird dog.

## 5. Jordan Belfort: the Straight Line

Every sale is a straight line from "hello" to "yes". Anything that pulls you off the line (small talk that goes nowhere, technical rambling) you bring back gently.

**The Three Tens.** Before someone buys, they need to be at 10 out of 10 on three certainties:
1. They love the **product** (Lexi works).
2. They trust **you**.
3. They trust the **company** behind it (Uncle T Agency is real and won't disappear).

When someone says "let me think about it", one of the three is below 10. Find which one and fix that one, don't repeat the whole pitch.

**Tonality.** Most of what you communicate is in *how* you say it. Practise saying your price like it's the most normal thing in the world. If you flinch on "R499 a month", they flinch too.

**Looping.** When they object, answer it, then loop back through the value and ask again. Not three times in a row aggressively. Once, warmly, then ask.

## 6. Chris Voss: negotiating like an FBI hostage negotiator

From *Never Split the Difference*. The best tools for handling a nervous small-business owner.

- **Mirroring.** Repeat their last 1 to 3 words as a question. "It's too expensive?" Then shut up. They'll explain what they really mean.
- **Labelling.** Name their feeling. "It sounds like you've been burned by tech guys before." Watch their shoulders drop.
- **Accusation audit.** Say the worst thing they're thinking before they do. "You're probably thinking I'm another guy selling an app that'll stop working in two months."
- **Calibrated questions.** "How" and "what" questions that make them solve your problem. "What would need to happen for this to make sense for you?"
- **Get to "That's right."** When they say "that's right" (not "you're right"), they feel understood. That's when they buy.
- **No-oriented questions.** People feel safer saying no. "Would it be a bad idea to try it for a month?" gets more yeses than "Do you want to try it?"

## 7. Jeb Blount: an empty pipeline is the real problem

From *Fanatical Prospecting*.

- **The 30-Day Rule.** Prospecting you do this month pays over the next 90 days. Skip a week now, you'll feel it in February.
- **The Law of Need.** The more desperate you are for one deal, the more likely you lose it. Desperation smells. The cure is a full pipeline, so no single "no" matters.
- **Golden hours.** Protect a fixed daily block for prospecting only. For you: lunch break at work (WhatsApp outreach) and Saturday morning (walk-ins).
- **Short, frequent, many channels.** WhatsApp, a walk-in, a voice note, a comment on their post. Not one long message.

## 8. Grant Cardone: volume and follow-up

- **The 10X Rule.** Set targets ten times bigger than feels comfortable, then take ten times the action. Most people's goals fail because the effort was planned too small.
- **Agree, then close.** Never argue with an objection. "I hear you, and that's exactly why..." then ask for the sale again.
- **Follow up like it's your job.** Most of your deals won't close on the first visit. A lead that said "maybe" gets a follow-up every 3 to 5 days until it's a yes or a clear no.
- **Price is rarely the real issue.** "Too expensive" usually means "I'm not sure it'll work for me". Go back to proof.

## 9. Zig Ziglar: the foundation under all of it

> "You can have everything in life you want, if you will just help enough other people get what they want."

**The five reasons people don't buy:** no need, no money, no hurry, no desire, no trust. Every objection you ever hear is one of these five. Learn to spot which one, and you know what to fix:

| They say | It's really | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm fine with my paper diary" | No need | Show them a missed booking costs R350+, ask how many WhatsApps go unanswered a week |
| "Money's tight" | No money (or no trust) | Break it down: R499 a month is R16 a day, less than one missed blow-dry |
| "Maybe after December" | No hurry | December is your busiest month, that's when you lose the most bookings |
| "I don't really need tech" | No desire | Sell Sundays off, not tech |
| "Let me think about it" | No trust | Proof: live Chales demo, a refund guarantee, Chanté's phone number |

## The Uncle T method (all nine combined into one sales conversation)

1. **State** (Robbins): 2 minutes before you walk in, get your energy right.
2. **Rapport** (Robbins + Girard): their language, their pace, something real and local.
3. **Accusation audit** (Voss): "You've probably had guys like me selling you stuff before."
4. **Clarify and label** (Hormozi + Voss): get them to name the pain. Wait for "that's right".
5. **Demo** (Hormozi likelihood): take out your phone, WhatsApp the demo number, let them watch it book.
6. **Offer** (Hormozi grand slam): price said calmly (Belfort tonality), guarantee, bonuses.
7. **Objection** (Ziglar five + Voss mirror + Belfort three tens): find which certainty is low, fix that one.
8. **Close with a no-oriented question** (Voss): "Would it be crazy to get you live before December rush?"
9. **Reinforce and ask for a referral** (Hormozi + Girard): "Who else do you know who's drowning in WhatsApps?"
10. **Follow up** (Cardone + Blount): no answer means follow up in 3 days, then weekly.

Books to buy, in this order: *$100M Offers* (Hormozi), *Never Split the Difference* (Voss), *$100M Leads* (Hormozi), *How to Sell Anything to Anybody* (Girard), *Fanatical Prospecting* (Blount). Hormozi's books are free as audiobooks on his YouTube channel.
