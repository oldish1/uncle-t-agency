# Research: engineering and fabrication websites (20 Oct 2026)

Sites read with Firecrawl (text and screenshots): Pegasus Steel (Germiston), CFW Projects (Cape Town and Johannesburg), SendCutSend, Protolabs, Xometry, TRUMPF, Bystronic. Screenshots: outputs/2026-10-20-research-sa-sites.png and outputs/2026-10-20-research-world-sites.png.

## What each one does
- **Pegasus Steel (SA, laser cutting and steel processing).** Headline: "Precision Steel Cutting & Fabrication, Delivered at Scale, On Time Anywhere in South Africa." Drone footage of the plant as the hero. A row of proof badges directly under it: ISO 9001:2015, B-BBEE Level 3, satisfaction guarantee, 1,250+ tons processed a month, 24/7 operation. Phone and email bar at the very top. One action everywhere: "Request a Free Estimate", with "Download Company Profile" as the soft second action. Then services, industries served, testimonials and a 10-question FAQ (maximum thickness, lead time, minimum orders, accuracy, cost, delivery).
- **CFW Projects (SA, turnkey equipment and automation).** Slider hero, three project cards with real photos of the equipment, "why turnkey", "tried and trusted", offices in Cape Town and Johannesburg. Dated look, strong real product photography.
- **SendCutSend (USA).** One promise: "Fast custom parts, at any scale", delivered in as little as 24 hours. Dark video hero, one red button. Proof: 100,000+ companies. Public price examples.
- **Protolabs and Xometry (USA).** The whole first screen is one action: get an instant quote. Certifications under it, then big numbers (buyers, suppliers, parts shipped) and customer logos.
- **TRUMPF and Bystronic (machine makers).** Large photography with people and finished parts, small navigation, one block per machine type ("Your best choice for laser cutting / press brakes").

## What they all share
1. One plain promise in the headline.
2. One main button, repeated: get a quote or estimate.
3. A proof row straight under the first screen: certifications, tonnage, years, customer logos.
4. Real pictures of real work.
5. An industries-we-serve section.
6. A FAQ with real answers (thickness, lead time, minimum order). It answers buyers and feeds Google.
7. Phone and email always visible.
8. A company profile PDF as a soft second action.

## Where T & T stands (v4, 18 Oct)
Ahead: real machine footage, scroll-driven machine story, smoother feel than all the South African sites, after-hours quote form, hours.
Missing: a proof row, industries served, a FAQ, a company profile PDF, a gallery of finished work, certifications, and capacity facts.

## Facts needed from T & T before building these (never invent)
ISO or other certificates; B-BBEE level; laser max thickness and bed size; press brake max thickness and tooling; typical lead time; delivery or collection; minimum order; industries and a few customers who agree to be named; real photos of finished work.

## Rule
Mirror the structure and the standard, never copy text, images or logos. The twist for T & T: real machine footage that plays as you scroll, a family workshop since 2001 under Errol Jeftha, and quotes taken at any hour.

## Round two: ten more (screenshots: outputs/2026-10-20-research-world2a.png and world2b.png)
Q Industrial (Awwwards site of the day), Summit Steel Works (steel fabricator, Awwwards nominee), CML Engineering (Awwwards nominee), Anduril, Vermeer, Nucor, Redwood Materials, GE Aerospace, Formlabs, JLG.

What stood out
- **Summit Steel Works** is the closest match to T & T: black ground, one orange accent, huge condensed caps, a real photograph of a worker at a machine as the hero, "Fabricating the future", clear sections for what they do and why they are different.
- **JLG, Vermeer, SSW** put a real person in the hero. **Nucor** shows welding sparks in a real photo. **Redwood and GE Aerospace** open on full-screen film. **Anduril** opens on a dark silhouette. **Q Industrial** opens on one 3D product object.
- **Vermeer** adds a "browse by industry" block and a "find a dealer" bar, so a buyer finds their own case in two clicks.
- **Formlabs** shows each product as a card with its key specs.
- Headlines are short. Heavy industry mostly uses bold condensed capitals with one accent colour (orange or yellow).
- None of the strongest sites crowd the first screen. One image or film, one sentence, one button.

Lessons for T & T
1. The hero should be a real person at a real machine, or real machine footage, with one sentence and one button.
2. Show people. A face earns trust faster than any badge.
3. Add an industries-we-serve block so a buyer can find themselves.
4. Show capacity as spec cards (laser, press brake, punch), like product cards.
5. Keep the first screen simple.
