# Research: traffic, competitors, pricing and voice (3 Nov 2026)

Sources read with Firecrawl on 3 Nov: Growth Experts' Core Four write-up (Hormozi), Gary Vaynerchuk's "Document, Don't Create" post (Aug 2026), a Liam Ottley panel of agency owners on getting a first client, BrightLocal's 2026 local-search statistics, Fresha's pricing page, the Calendesk Setmore vs Fresha comparison, Allo's 2026 review of AI receptionists for salons, Meta's WhatsApp Business pricing page (updated 30 Sep 2026) and a MyBroadband report on the South African rates. Names in the request were read as Alex Hormozi, Gary Vaynerchuk (Gary Vee), Liam Ottley, Fresha and Setmore.

## 1. How the big names get traffic: there is no secret, there is a system

**Hormozi's Core Four**, in this order: (1) warm outreach to people who already know you, (2) cold outreach to strangers, (3) post free content, (4) paid ads. Ads come last. Writers who use it say to start ads small, only after the first three prove the message. The edge is not a trick. It is volume, every day, for a long time, and a strong offer.

**Gary Vee, "Document, don't create":** don't try to make the perfect video. Film what you actually do and post it. He says to post six or seven times a day, which suits a media company. A one-person business can do one a day.

**Liam Ottley and the agency owners on his panel:**
- Pick a niche and get known for it.
- Tell your network what you do. People think of you when the problem comes up.
- Take testimonial clients for free or heavily discounted at the start, so there is proof.
- Content is like buying property: it gets more valuable over time, and the people looking for the thing you do come to you.
- A cold system and content are different bets: cold gives measurable replies fast, content builds over months.

**What this means for us (honest read):** these people sell to online audiences around the world, so content can go viral for them. Our buyers are local owners who run on WhatsApp and word of mouth. So for us: warm outreach, in-person and WhatsApp first, then local content that proves Lexi works, then Google Maps and reviews, then small ads. Chasing "an insane amount of traffic" is the wrong goal. The right goal is conversations with the right owners. Their own numbers say a few hundred right visitors beat thousands of wrong ones.

**Local search facts (BrightLocal 2026, mostly US data, treat as a guide):** 84% of consumers searched for a local business in the last 3 months. 73% of local searches start on a phone. 75% choose a business within 30 minutes and 72% look at three or fewer. Business websites are the top source people check (47%), then directories (31%). Takeaway: have a clear profile, reviews and a site that loads fast on a phone.

## 2. Competitors and pricing

**Booking software (no voice, no WhatsApp conversation):**
- Fresha: $19.95 a month per business plus $14.95 per team member, 20% one-time fee on brand-new clients found through its marketplace (minimum $6), card fees about 2.3 to 2.8% + $0.20. About R370 a month for the base plan at roughly R18.5 to the dollar.
- Setmore: paid plans from $12 a month per user. Reminders by SMS and email are on higher plans.
These are diaries with online booking. A customer has to open a link. Neither answers a WhatsApp message like a person.

**AI phone receptionists (voice, mostly US):** Allo from $18 a month (flat, unlimited AI answering); Rosie $49 to $299 a month by minutes; Goodcall $79 to $249 per agent; Quo Sona $19 per user for 10 calls then $0.75 a call; Ooma add-on $14.99. In South Africa, BizAI's Voice Valet is R999 a month for about 200 minutes (from our 25 Sept research).

**Where Lexi sits:** nobody in our local market combines a WhatsApp chat assistant and a voice assistant on one number with calendar booking, reminders, cancel and reschedule, set up and supported by a local person who speaks Afrikaans. That is the gap. Our price (R799 a month for Lexi, R999 with a website) is above Fresha's base plan, so we must sell what Fresha does not do: answers customers for you, at night, on WhatsApp.

## 3. Voice (from the 25 Sept research plus today)
- WhatsApp calling is the best fit: calls clients make to the business are free from Meta's side, same number and same Lexi brain. Only the AI voice platform costs money, about R6 to R8 for a 3-minute booking call (Bland, Vapi, Retell).
- Keep "Allow voice calls" off in WhatsApp Manager until the AI is connected.
- Price as an add-on to Lexi of roughly R500 to R900 a month with minutes included, decided after a real cost test. Sell it as "Lexi answers chats and calls", not a separate product.
- Not verified: how well any platform handles South African English or Afrikaans. Test on our own number before promising it.

## 4. WhatsApp costs changed on 1 October 2026 (important)
Meta's pricing page (updated 30 Sep 2026) says: every business phone number gets 1,000 free service messages a month; from the 1,001st the number is charged, and messages stop being delivered after the free tier unless a payment method is on the account in Billing Hub. MyBroadband reports South African rates of about R0.12 a utility or service message and R0.62 a marketing message; check the exact rate card in Billing Hub before quoting it. Messages a customer sends to you are free. Replies inside a "click to WhatsApp" ad window are free for the first 72 hours.
- A booking chat is about 8 to 10 replies, so 1,000 free messages is roughly 100 to 120 bookings a month per number. Fine for a small salon, but worth counting for a busy one.
- Action: add a payment method for the WhatsApp numbers (Chales, the demo number) or Lexi can go silent after 1,000 messages in a month.
- Decide how extra message costs are handled with clients (included up to a fair limit, then passed on at cost).
