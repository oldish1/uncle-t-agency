# Research: what makes a site look professional and get shared (17 Oct 2026)

Sources read with Firecrawl: Hon Tran (Awwwards juror) "Best award-winning websites 2026", Figma's 13 web design trends for 2026, Utsubo's guide to storytelling websites, HorizonX (explore page and template descriptions), plus live screenshots of horizonx.so, Linear, by-kin.com and the Awwwards Sites of the Day grid. Mobbin's landing page gallery needs a paid plan, so it was not used.

## What jurors say separates a winner from a "nice" site

- **Art direction.** One idea behind every type, colour and grid choice. Remove the motion and the still frame should still look designed.
- **Directed motion.** Scroll sequences that pace a story, transitions that carry meaning, micro-interactions on controls. Choreography, not effects.
- **Performance.** 60fps on a mid-range phone. Heavy 3D that stutters loses.
- **A reduced-motion path.** Real craftspeople build one.

## What the best looks like when you look at it

- **The type is small, tight and confident.** Linear's hero headline is about 48px with tight tracking, in one sans, with muted grey support text. HorizonX's is about the same. Award studios set small text and let imagery lead. Oversized headlines are the beginner move.
- **The product or the picture is the hero.** Linear shows the real interface big under a short headline. Award sites open on a full image or a scene.
- **One cursor-reactive or scroll-reactive hero object.** HorizonX's best templates open on WebGL particle figures that scatter under the pointer, a floating 3D object over a live scene, or a 157-frame scroll sequence.
- **Scroll storytelling in five scenes at most**, ending on one action (Utsubo).
- **Bento grids** for features, on near-black, one idea per tile.
- **Dark, restrained palette with one accent**, grain or glow for depth, not clutter.
- **Smooth scroll** (Lenis) so everything feels weighted.
- **Black or white pill buttons**, small nav, no clutter.

## What it means for Uncle Ts Automation

- Type: Inter 500 with tight tracking, headline 40 to 58px, body 15px. Bodoni Moda italic only for one accent word.
- Hero: a particle Table Mountain in WhatsApp green that scatters under the cursor, with the playable Lexi phone in front.
- Story: a pinned scroll scene, "a day with Lexi", where the clock runs 6am to 9pm, chats appear and the calendar fills.
- Features as a bento grid of small live visuals, not paragraphs.
- Smooth scroll, grain, magnetic buttons, a rolling view transition, and a reduced-motion path for all of it.

## Round two: Studio and Start views (research and plan)

Sources: Growform's study of 114 multi-step forms (the 9 ingredients), Figma's about-page examples and tips, Shorthand's scrollytelling examples, Awwwards parallax and GSAP collections.

What the sources say:
- **About pages** win when they connect the story to the visitor. Local context and a warm serif carry trust for small businesses. Too many are self-absorbed or jargon.
- **Scrollytelling** should serve the story, not decorate it. Five scenes at most, one idea each.
- **Scroll-fill text** (words go from muted to full ink as you scroll) and **pinned horizontal panels** are the standard award-site moves for story sections.
- **Forms:** a first question that is a tap, a reason for each thing asked, their own answers used back at them, a truthful progress bar, the phone number last with a reason, and a named person with a timeframe.

Plan:
- **Studio (light):** a statement hero with a live Cape Town clock, Theo's story as a scroll-fill paragraph, "Four days" as a pinned horizontal run of drawn panels with a progress bar, suburbs as a big hover list, smooth-opening FAQ.
- **Start (dark):** a full-bleed pinned Table Mountain that runs dawn, noon, dusk and night with the sun arcing across and Lexi's messages at each hour; a conversational enquiry wizard (business, need, name and suburb) with a live WhatsApp message building beside it; the first client as a short text; details and a closing action.
- Reduced motion shows static versions of all of it.

## Round three: Mobbin public pages (25 Oct 2026)

Source: mobbin.com public section pages (hero, pricing, social proof, stats, about, showcase), read through Firecrawl screenshots. The full library and its MCP need a paid plan, so only the public grid was studied. Mobbin's image servers are blocked in the cloud container, so screenshots of the pages were used.

What the best examples do:
- **Hero:** a short headline (4 to 7 words) in one confident face, one product shot or visual that does real work (an actual interface, a photo with a floating UI card), and a trust strip of logos or one proof number directly underneath. Titan and Ease pair a headline block with a stats row. ISO Meet floats a small UI card over a photograph.
- **Social proof:** one big quote with three large stat tiles (70%, 4x, 64%) under a row of logos. Numbers are huge, labels tiny.
- **Pricing:** three plans, the recommended one carries a "Most popular" badge and a highlighted border or glow, every plan lists what it includes as ticks, price is the biggest thing in the card, and a billing toggle or one calm "contact us" row sits underneath.
- **Style tags that win:** minimal, light or dark with one accent, scroll effects, glass, photography. Almost none use heavy decoration.

What we apply to Uncle Ts Automation:
- Pricing: "Most popular" badge on Lexi plus website, ticks for what is included, glowing border kept.
- A proof band: three big figures (24/7, 4 days, 2 to 5 sec) already exist on Start; add a row of "built for" business types under the Home hero.
- No fake logos or invented numbers, ever. Only real facts.
