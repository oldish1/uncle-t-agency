# The offer

## The founding offer (locked 2026-09-22, first 5 clients only)

**"The Founding 5": Lexi + website for R1,000 a month.** These first 5 are the testimonial clients: they get the easy yes, and Theo gets proof to sell the rest.

- R0 setup (normal price R1,500 once-off).
- They pay nothing until Lexi has booked their first 10 clients (changed from 20 on 2026-09-22 so Theo gets paid sooner; a busy salon hits 10 in about a week). If she hasn't booked 10 in the first 30 days, it's switched off and there's no bill.
- If Lexi ever double-books them, that month is free.
- Month to month, no contract, cancel with one WhatsApp. Price locked at R1,000 for as long as they stay.
- Includes: Lexi on WhatsApp (English and Afrikaans, day and night, books into Google Calendar, reminders), a mobile website with a "Book on WhatsApp" button, domain and hosting, Google Business Profile cleaned up, a QR code poster for the door, and new features as they ship (deposits, cancel/reschedule, review requests).
- Covers one diary and up to about 120 bookings a month. Extra stylist diaries R200 each (suggested, not yet tested on a client).
- Paid monthly by Yoco payment link, EFT or PayShap. No debit orders for founding clients.
- In return: a voice note or video testimonial after month one, a Google review, and introductions to two other owners.
- Lexi introduces herself as the salon's booking assistant. She never pretends to be a person.

## After the first 5 (from client 6)

Once 5 salons have Lexi working and testimonials are in, every new client pays (confirmed by Theo, 2026-09-23):

- **R1,500 once-off setup**, then **R1,000 a month** for Lexi plus the website, from go-live.
- No "pay after 10 bookings", because the testimonials and the demo video are the proof now.
- Month to month, so the setup fee is the only commitment.

**Still open:** a R599 "Lexi Solo" tier for one-chair braiders and nail techs (researched, not approved yet).

## Products and services

**The Lexi System**, the flagship. A WhatsApp AI booking assistant that runs on her own dedicated WhatsApp number, separate from the owner's personal number. The client puts that number on their website, socials and cards. Greets the customer, lists services, takes date and time, checks live calendar availability so nothing double-books, confirms, and sends reminders to cut no-shows. Sold as "a receptionist who never sleeps and never double-books," never as "a chatbot" or "AI technology," the pitch leads with the pain (missed messages at night, no-shows, double bookings, the owner personally fielding WhatsApp between clients), not the tech.

Old standard price (before the founding offer, now replaced): R1,499 once-off setup, R499/month recurring. Lexi and the website are now sold together as one bundle: R1,500 setup + R1,000/month. Rollout: 4 days, build, connect, test, live.

**Websites.** A single mobile-first HTML site, built and hosted, R799 once-off setup, then R499/month with hosting and domain included. Lexi plus a website together is R1,499 setup and R799/month. Positioned directly against Telecloud, who charge R2,000 to R11,000 once-off for the same tier of work and bill hosting separately.

**Not yet built, in the queue once Lexi is stable and there's real paying volume:** follow-up automation, Google review request automation, client reactivation campaigns. These are deliberately held back so no client is sold something unproven.

**Parked, not active:** an Android SMS gateway add-on (revisit once there are 5+ paying clients), an SA-adapted AI bookkeeping product (CSV bank exports through Make.com and the Claude API into Sheets and WhatsApp, multilingual, researched but not built).

## How it's sold

Direct outreach, WhatsApp and in-person, not paid ads. A 20-prospect Cape Town salon list runs through Google Sheets with a Make.com daily email automation on top, plus WhatsApp messages sent directly.

The strategy is five founding clients at a discounted rate in exchange for a testimonial and one trackable success metric each, banked as sales proof before any upsell gets offered. CHALES Hair Boutique is the live proof-of-concept client, referenceable and demoable right now.

Warm leads in the pipeline: Zanzibar Hair and Beauty (replied), Sistergirl (asked about pricing), Reeva Hair, JEM Hair and Beauty Studio, and Jason, a tattoo artist at No Love Lost, referred in through Theo's sister.


## Founding five deal (decided 25 Oct 2026)

For the first five paying salons: first 30 days free, then setup R999 (normal R1,499) and R499 a month. If she cancels in the first month she owes nothing. Estimated real cost to us per salon for the free month: R100 to R150 (see `plans/2026-10-25-game-plan-pricing-chales-google.md`).

**Positioning:** Lexi is the salon's personal assistant, not software. "Get found. Get booked. Get remembered." Competitors (Fresha, Booksy, Setmore) sell a tool the owner has to run; Lexi takes the WhatsApp work off the owner. Proof: Chales, about 100 real bookings since 10 June, about 34 clients.

**After the founders** (draft, not final): Starter R499 (Lexi bookings and reminders), Plus R799 (adds review requests, win-back messages, memberships), Grow R1,299 (adds Google profile care, monthly results report, website).

## The needle (29 Oct 2026)

Competitor reviews show the loudest complaints are surprise fees, commission on new clients, and support that disappears (`reference/research/2026-10-29-competitor-complaints.md`). Our line: flat price, no commission, your clients stay yours in your own WhatsApp, and a real person answers. Use it on the website, in follow-ups and in demos. State it as fact, never attack a competitor by name. Don't promise "unlimited" bookings until cost per booking is measured.

**Correction (29 Oct, South Africa check):** flat price and no commission are not unique here (Booklink is R79 flat, Salonz R199). The needle that holds in South Africa is: every other tool sells a booking link; Lexi answers the WhatsApp conversation itself, in the owner's own number, voice notes included. Lead with that. Price risk: R499 against R79 to R199, so justify it with bookings captured. Details: `reference/research/2026-10-29-competitor-complaints.md`.
